Trader Mayne. Trading Series — Episode 8 — Order Blocks vs. Fair Value Gaps (Selection Framework)

\---

\*\*1. Core Concept\*\*

This video teaches how to decide which point of interest to use — a fair value gap (FVG) or an order block (OB) — when both are present in the same dealing range, using a four-filter framework anchored by location and low-time-frame confirmation.

\---

\*\*2. Key Principles\*\*

\#\#\# Quick Recap: FVG vs. OB Definitions

\- \*\*Fair Value Gap (FVG):\*\* Three-candle pattern. Bullish \= take high of candle 1, low of candle 3 — gap in between, with upward single-direction displacement. Bearish \= inverse. Requires clear displacement through the zone.  
\- \*\*Order Block (OB):\*\* The last opposing candle (or candles) before a big displacement move that breaks market structure (MSB). Bullish OB \= last down candle before a large move up. Bearish OB \= last up candle before a large move down. Best OBs: take out a prior low/high, cause an MSB, and create their own FVG.  
\- A good OB almost always creates an FVG. They are structurally linked — the OB creates the displacement that becomes the FVG. The FVG will therefore always sit structurally ahead of (or directly on top of) the OB in price delivery.

\#\#\# The Three Structural Relationships Between OB and FVG

These three configurations determine which filtering approach applies:

| Relationship | Description | Implication |  
|---|---|---|  
| \*\*Separated\*\* | FVG forms with clear price space above (bullish) or below (bearish) the OB | Requires full filter cascade to determine which one holds |  
| \*\*Stacked / Touching\*\* | FVG immediately abuts or connects with the outer boundary of the OB candle | Zones are adjacent; LTF confirmation becomes the decisive filter |  
| \*\*Overlapping ("Unicorn")\*\* | FVG, OB, and Breaker Block occupy the exact same price space | Highest-probability, highest hit-rate setup in the system — alarm bells |

\#\#\# The Four Filters

\*\*Filter 1 — Location (Most Important)\*\*

\- If bullish: look only for PDAs/POIs in the \*\*discount\*\* (below the 50% / EQ of the dealing range). Ignore anything in the premium — it may work, but it is low probability.  
\- If bearish: look only for PDAs/POIs in the \*\*premium\*\*. Ignore those in the discount.  
\- Ideal bearish location: the 61.8–78.6% OTE region (not just barely above 50%).  
\- The FVG will typically appear \*\*above\*\* the OB in a bullish setup (FVG is formed by the displacement that originates from the OB). Always the case structurally.

\*\*Filter 2 — Context (Market Condition)\*\*

| Market Condition | Preferred Tool | Rationale |  
|---|---|---|  
| Aggressive trend | FVG | Shallow pullbacks — price often doesn't reach the OB |  
| Ranging / sideways | OB | FVGs overlap each other in choppy price action; OBs are cleaner |  
| Reversal / putting in a high or low | OB \+ Breaker | These structures dominate at swing highs/lows |

\*\*Filter 3 — Quality\*\*

\- FVG quality markers: size of displacement (larger \= higher quality); occurs after an OB; occurs after an MSB.  
\- OB quality markers: took out a prior low (bullish) or prior high (bearish); caused an MSB; created its own FVG (displacement). A small candle OB with no prior-low sweep \= lower quality.  
\- Rule: "It should be obvious." If you're second-guessing whether it qualifies, it probably doesn't.

\*\*Filter 4 — Risk/Reward\*\*

\- The FVG is structurally higher (closer to current price) than the OB. If entering at the FVG doesn't produce a minimum 2R to target, skip it and wait for the OB.  
\- If neither the FVG nor the OB produces acceptable RR, pass on the trade entirely.

\#\#\# The Ultimate Filter — Low Time Frame Confirmation

\- High time frame (weekly/daily): identifies the dealing range, marks the OB and FVG as the zone.  
\- Low time frame (your entry TF): when price reaches the HTF zone, you will be in a \*\*counter-trend\*\* on the LTF (uptrend on HTF \= downtrend on LTF as it pulls back into discount).  
\- \*\*What you're looking for:\*\* wait for the LTF downtrend to produce a \*\*market structure shift (MSS/MSB)\*\* — flip from downtrend to uptrend. That's your confirmation signal.  
\- If the MSS happens at the FVG level → enter at the FVG.  
\- If no MSS at FVG and price continues down to the OB → look for MSS at the OB.  
\- If no MSS at either → do not enter; potential trend reversal underway.  
\- Entry tools on the LTF after the MSS: breaker blocks, OBs, or FVGs on the entry timeframe.

\#\#\# Invalidation Cascade

When a POI fails to hold, the logic shifts systematically rather than randomly:

1\. FVG aggressively traded through without LTF structure change → shift focus to the underlying OB.  
2\. OB aggressively traded through without LTF structure change → a macro market structure shift is likely underway; do not force an entry, reassess HTF thesis entirely.

\#\#\# Overlap \= Highest Probability ("Unicorn" Setup)

\- When an OB, FVG, and breaker block all occur at the \*\*exact same price level\*\*, this is the highest-probability setup in the system.  
\- Called "unicorn setups" in the ICT/smart money community.  
\- When OB and FVG overlap, "alarm bells go off" — this is the setup to prioritize above all others.

\#\#\# Mayne's Personal System Best Practice

\- High time frame setups: uses \*\*OBs and breakers\*\* primarily; rarely enters on HTF FVGs.  
\- Low time frame entries: uses \*\*breakers\*\* as the primary entry tool.  
\- FVGs work well on micro timeframes (5m, 15m) in trending conditions, tagging sequentially along the trend.  
\- The three tools he uses exclusively: \*\*Order Blocks, Breakers, Fair Value Gaps\*\*. All others (mitigation blocks, inverse FVGs, vacuum blocks, etc.) are dismissed as unnecessary.

\*\*4. Execution Checklist\*\*

1\. \*\*Mark your HTF dealing range\*\* (weekly or daily). Identify EQ (50%).  
2\. \*\*Determine bias\*\* (bullish or bearish). If bullish, only focus on PDAs in the discount.  
3\. \*\*Mark both the OB and FVG\*\* within the discount zone. Note the structural relationship: Separated, Stacked, or Overlapping. Overlapping \= highest priority immediately.  
4\. \*\*Apply the context filter:\*\* Is the market trending aggressively (favor FVG), ranging (favor OB), or reversing (favor OB \+ breaker)?  
5\. \*\*Apply the quality filter:\*\* Is the OB high-quality (swept prior low, MSB, created FVG)? Is the FVG large and obvious with clear displacement?  
6\. \*\*Apply the RR filter:\*\* At the FVG entry price, does the trade produce minimum 2R to target? If not, wait for OB or pass.  
7\. \*\*When price reaches the HTF zone, drop to your entry timeframe.\*\* You will be in a counter-trend. Wait — do not enter yet.  
8\. \*\*Look for the LTF market structure shift (MSS).\*\* If it occurs at the FVG level → enter using LTF tools (breaker, OB, or FVG on entry TF). If not, wait for price to reach the OB level and repeat.  
9\. \*\*If the FVG fails\*\* (aggressively traded through without LTF structure change) → shift focus to the OB.  
10\. \*\*If the OB also fails\*\* → do not force an entry; a macro structure shift is likely underway. Reassess the HTF thesis entirely.  
11\. \*\*Overlap check:\*\* If OB \+ FVG (+ breaker) are stacked at the same level, this is the highest-priority setup — size up or prioritize accordingly.

\*\*The Cheat Sheet\*\*

\*\*The Rule:\*\* When price reaches a HTF zone with both an FVG and an OB, don't guess — drop to the low time frame and wait for a market structure shift. If the LTF flips at the FVG level, enter there. If it doesn't flip until the OB, enter there. If it never flips, stay out. If either zone gets aggressively traded through without a structural flip, follow the invalidation cascade — FVG fails → OB; OB fails → step aside entirely.
