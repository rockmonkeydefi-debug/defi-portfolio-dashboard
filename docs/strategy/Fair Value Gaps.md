## **Core Concept**

A Fair Value Gap (FVG) is a three-candle price pattern that marks a zone of one-directional price inefficiency \- created when a large displacement candle moves price so aggressively that the market leaves an imbalance, which it will often return to fill before continuing the original move. Because the market seeks efficiency and balance, price frequently returns to mitigate this structural void before resuming its original trajectory.

## **Key Principles**

* **Structure:** Three consecutive candles required \- candle 1 (regular), candle 2 (displacement/middle), candle 3 (regular).  
* **Displacement requirement:** The middle candle must exhibit sharp, clear displacement \- a large body relative to its wicks, illustrating rapid expanding momentum in one direction.   
* **Color discretion:** Candles 1 and 3 can be any color \- they are entirely interchangeable. Only the displacement candle's direction matters.  
* **Potency scale:** The physical size of the displacement candle dictates setup quality on a spectrum.  
  * Larger, more explosive middle candles create structurally superior FVGs.  
  * Small displacement candles produce valid but lower-probability setups.  
* **Why price returns:** Markets seek efficiency — every buyer has a seller, the market is zero sum. Price blew through the FVG zone so quickly in one direction that it left an imbalance. The market will often come and fill that inefficiency at a later time before moving on. Mayne's analogy: an elastic band stretched very quickly — the snapback to the FVG is the entry, then continuation of the original move.  
* **Not all FVGs get filled.** Not all that get filled get respected. Context determines which are high-probability.

## **FVG Use Cases**

* **High-probability FVG criteria (bullish)**  
  * Occurs in the discount of the dealing range (below equilibrium/50%)  
  * Formed after sweeping sellside liquidity (e.g., a run on a major low or an SFP)  
  * HTF Bullish market structure  
  * Large displacement candle  
  * Ideally nested within or adjacent to a HTF order block or POI  
* **High-probability FVG criteria (bearish)**  
  * Occurs in the premium of the dealing range (below equilibrium/50%)  
  * Formed after buyside liquidity sweep  (e.g., a run on a major high or an SFP)  
  * HTF bearish market structure  
  * Large displacement candle  
  * Ideally nested within or adjacent to a HTF order block or POI  
* **Low-probability FVG:** In the wrong zone (e.g., bullish FVG forming in the premium), no HTF alignment, small displacement candle.  
* **Dual use:** An FVG can function as  
  * A direct entry trigger on the current timeframe  
  * A zone to drop down to a lower timeframe and look for a lower-timeframe setup (e.g., another FVG or market structure shift) forming within it.  
* **Entry mechanics:**  
  * Bullish FVG: enter at or within the upper 50% of the gap zone  
  * Bearish FVG: enter at or within the lower 50% of the gap zone (i.e., the first 50% price reaches on the return)  
  * Can enter right at the edge or ladder in (spray orders) across the relevant 50% zone  
* **Stop Loss**  
  * **Option 1 (Aggressive / Tight R:R):** Directly beyond the FVG boundary; Best risk/reward but if price breaches the full box, stopped immediately.  
    * The logic: a good FVG should completely support price and not be traded through at all.  
  * **Option 2 (Conservative / Structural):** Beyond candle 1 of the three-candle pattern; more breathing room for internal liquidity sweeps before continuation.  
    * Gets stopped out less frequently, but risk/reward worsens and position sizing must be reduced accordingly.  
  * **Stop choice is discretionary:** It depends on the setup, time frame, confidence level, and quality of the context leading up to it.  
* **Target:** External range liquidity \- the next significant high or low beyond the current dealing range.  
  * Price moves from internal range liquidity (the FVG entry) toward external range liquidity (the target).

## **Execution Checklist**

* Determine HTF market structure direction (bullish or bearish) — start at the weekly or daily.  
* Mark the dealing range: significant low to significant high.  
* Identify the equilibrium (50% level). Locate discount zone for longs, premium zone for shorts.  
* Map liquidity: where is sellside/buyside? Internal vs. external range? Has a sweep occurred?  
* Wait for price to reach the relevant zone (discount for longs, premium for shorts).  
* On a lower timeframe (daily, H4, or lower per HTF cadence), look for a three-candle FVG pattern forming within that zone.  
* Confirm before proceeding: large displacement candle (middle), correct candle 2 color, zone is in discount/premium per HTF bias, liquidity sweep has occurred or is concurrent (e.g., SFP), and optionally that the zone overlaps with an HTF order block or POI.  
* Draw the FVG box: high of candle 1 to low of candle 3 (bullish) or low of candle 1 to high of candle 3 (bearish).  
* Set an alert for price to return and tag the FVG.  
* On the return: enter at or within the relevant 50% of the FVG (upper 50% for bullish, lower 50% for bearish). Can ladder in across that zone.  
* Set stop loss:  
  * **Option 1 (Aggressive):** directly outside the FVG boundary. Tighter, better R:R, higher stop-out rate.  
  * **Option 2 (Conservative):** outside candle 1 of the three-candle pattern. Wider, lower stop-out rate, worse R:R — size down accordingly.  
* Use discretion on stop choice based on setup quality and confidence.  
* Set target at external range liquidity.  
* Do not trade FVGs in isolation — full Phase 1 context is required for high-probability setups.

## **Open Questions / Ambiguities**

* **Filling vs. respecting:** Mayne distinguishes between an FVG getting filled (price returns to the zone) and getting respected (price reverses from it), but gives no precise filter at the moment of entry for predicting respect vs. blow-through beyond "context." No explicit criteria defined.  
* **Partial fills:** Mayne shows examples where price wicks into the FVG zone but does not trade through it entirely. He does not specify whether a partial fill counts as "filled" or what it means for the setup.  
* **Multiple FVGs on the same move:** He shows a real chart example where a new FVG forms immediately after the first is respected. No rules given for which FVG takes priority or how to manage overlapping FVGs on the same price leg.  
* **Timeframe selection:** He references weekly-to-daily-to-H12/H4 cadence but does not give a hard rule for which timeframe to use for the actual FVG entry. Framed as context-dependent.  
* **Phase 3 entry models:** Mayne repeatedly signals that Phase 3 will formalize FVG-based entry models in full. Until those are covered, the complete execution system remains partially defined.
